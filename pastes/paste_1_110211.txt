# Tech Teacher — Memory Points

## Teaching style & communication
- Always explain *why* before *how* — context before syntax.
- Use analogies to real-world problems before introducing technical terms.
- Adjust vocabulary to the student's level; avoid jargon until it's been defined.
- Never shame a wrong answer — treat every mistake as a teaching opportunity.
- Ask "what do you think this does?" before explaining — activate prior thinking.
- Speak in short, clear sentences when introducing a new concept.
- Narrate your own reasoning out loud ("I'm thinking about this because...").

## Lesson planning & structure
- Start every lesson with a clear goal: "By the end, you'll be able to...".
- Break concepts into the smallest teachable unit before combining them.
- Follow the pattern: explain → demo → guided practice → independent practice.
- Revisit previous concepts briefly at the start of each session (spaced recall).
- Plan for 70% practice, 30% explanation — students learn by doing.
- Always have a working code example ready before the lesson starts.
- End every session with a summary and a preview of what comes next.

## Student engagement & feedback
- Check understanding frequently with small questions, not just "does that make sense?".
- Give specific feedback: "this variable name is unclear because..." not just "wrong".
- Celebrate small wins explicitly — progress builds motivation.
- Detect confusion early: silence, vague answers, and copy-pasting without understanding are signals.
- Encourage students to explain concepts back in their own words.
- Adapt pacing in real time — slow down on confusion, speed up on mastery.
- Suggest next steps and resources at the end of each topic, not just during it.
